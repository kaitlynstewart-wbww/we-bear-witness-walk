Our Promise

You do not have to tell your story for us to stand beside you.

♥

The We Bear Witness Walk exists to stand beside survivors of sexual violence—not to ask them to relive what happened. Survivors never have to identify themselves, speak publicly, disclose their experience, or participate in media to belong here. You may attend quietly, bring someone you trust, use resources, walk, sit, or leave whenever you need.

Our Mission

Why We Bear Witness

Sexual violence often leaves people carrying experiences others may never see. Some survivors speak openly. Some tell one trusted person. Some never tell anyone. Some are still deciding what words to use. Every survivor deserves support.

We Bear Witness Walk was created to build a community where survivors can access advocacy, counseling, medical support, legal information, victim services, and connection without having to prove their pain first.

Our mission is not to speak for survivors. It is to listen when they choose to speak, respect silence when they do not, and make sure they know they do not have to walk alone.

Volunteer Standards

Volunteer Code of Conduct

Every volunteer helps protect the safety, dignity, privacy, and choice of the people attending We Bear Witness Walk. These expectations apply before, during, and after the event.

Protect privacy

Do not share names, stories, identifying details, conversations, or information you learn about attendees. Never ask someone whether they are a survivor.

Do not ask for details

If someone chooses to disclose an experience, listen without investigating, asking for proof, or pressing for details. Survivors decide what they share and when.

Believe. Listen. Offer Choice. Connect.

Thank the person for trusting you, listen without judgment, ask what would feel helpful, and connect them with a trained advocate or qualified professional when requested.

Stay within your role

General volunteers do not provide counseling, legal advice, medical advice, investigations, or promises of outcomes. Bring in the appropriate trained professional or event lead.

Respect physical boundaries

Do not hug, touch, guide, or physically assist someone without permission unless there is an immediate safety emergency.

Follow photography rules

No photography is permitted in Quiet Support, private advocate conversations, or First Aid areas. Identifiable photos elsewhere must follow the event's consent-based photography policy.

Use respectful, survivor-centered language

No victim blaming, jokes about abuse or sexual violence, minimizing comments, political arguments with attendees, harassment, discrimination, or pressure to participate.

Follow safety instructions

Follow directions from event leads, route marshals, medical personnel, public-safety partners, and trained advocates. Report safety concerns or boundary violations to your team lead promptly.

A disclosure is not an invitation to investigate. A volunteer's job is to be calm, respectful, and helpful—not to determine what happened or tell someone what they must do next.

From the Founder

A Note From Our Founder

Kaitlyn Stewart, Founder & Event Organizer
Kaitlyn is a survivor, wife, mother, and member of the Boerne community. She created We Bear Witness Walk to make support visible without asking survivors to publicly identify themselves or relive their trauma. Her hope is to bring practical resources, compassionate professionals, allies, and community support into one space where survivors can participate entirely on their own terms.

“I created We Bear Witness Walk as a survivor myself. I know how meaningful it can be simply to hear the words, ‘I believe you.’ I didn’t want to create an event that asks survivors to turn their trauma into a public story. I wanted to create one where the community does the work of showing up. My hope is that someone can stand quietly in Main Plaza—without telling another person why they’re there—and look around and understand that every person walking beside them is saying: I see you. I believe you. You are not alone.”
— Kaitlyn Stewart, Founder, We Bear Witness Walk
Privacy at the Event

Photography & Media Policy

We want attendees to be able to participate without feeling watched, identified, or pressured to appear in photos. No one has to disclose survivor status in order to receive privacy.

Identifiable photos require consent

Official photographers will ask before taking close-up or clearly identifiable portraits whenever practical. A person's presence at the event is not automatic permission to use their image.

Opt-in photo area

We plan to provide a clearly marked photo-friendly area for people who want portraits, group photos, or commemorative pictures. Privacy will not depend on wearing a special marker that could identify someone as a survivor.

Protected areas are camera-free

No photography or recording will be permitted in the Quiet Support Area, during private advocate or counselor conversations, in First Aid, or in other spaces designated for confidential support.

Children and teens

Identifiable portraits of minors by the official event photography team will require permission from a parent or legal guardian.

Attendee photography

Guests are asked not to photograph or record another attendee without permission. Staff may ask someone to stop photographing when privacy or safety is at risk.

Media and press

Media representatives must check in with event leadership, follow the same survivor-centered privacy rules, and may not enter protected support areas or approach attendees for survivor stories without clear consent.

Our preferred images: event signage, flowers, banners, resource tables, volunteers at work, speakers who have agreed to be photographed, hands holding signs, wide community scenes that minimize identifiable faces, and people who have actively opted into the photo area.
No survivor is required to be photographed, interviewed, or publicly identified. Choosing not to participate in photography will not affect access to the event, resources, support, or any other part of the day.

Final event-day photo procedures may be adjusted as the site plan, City requirements, and participating professional partners are confirmed.

Privacy & Form Data

Your privacy matters here.

We Bear Witness Walk does not require anyone to identify as a survivor or share details of sexual violence in order to attend, volunteer, partner, sponsor, ask a question, or request an accommodation.

Information you choose to submit

Our contact, volunteer, partnership, and sponsorship forms collect only the information you enter, such as your name, email address, phone number, organization, role preferences, availability, and message. We use that information to respond to your inquiry and coordinate event-related participation.

How forms are delivered

Website forms are processed through a third-party form service and then emailed to Kaitlyn Stewart, Founder & Event Organizer. Because form submissions pass through that service, please do not submit medical records, detailed trauma disclosures, client information, licensing documents, or other highly sensitive information through the website.

What we do not do

We do not sell form-submission information to advertisers. The website does not currently use advertising trackers or behavioral advertising. We do not use contact information to solicit survivor stories or require disclosure of survivor status.

Website hosting

The website is hosted through GitHub Pages. Like most web hosts, the hosting provider may process routine technical information needed to deliver and secure the site under its own policies.

Children and teen volunteers

Volunteer participation by minors may require parent or guardian involvement or consent. Children under 13 should not submit personal information through the volunteer form themselves; a parent or guardian should contact the organizer instead.

Correction or deletion requests

If you submitted information through the website and would like Kaitlyn to correct or delete the copy she received, email kepeel@yahoo.com. We will make reasonable efforts to honor the request for information under the organizer's control.

This website is not a confidential crisis or counseling service. If you need immediate survivor support, please use the support resources on this site rather than sending sensitive details through a form.

FAQ

Do I have to be a survivor to attend?

No. Survivors, loved ones, advocates, professionals, and allies are welcome.

Do survivors have to identify themselves?

No. No one will be asked to disclose or identify themselves as a survivor.

Do I have to walk?

No. You may stay at Main Plaza and participate in whatever way feels right for you.

Can allies attend?

Yes. Allies are encouraged to come listen, learn, and walk beside survivors.

Is the event free?

The event is planned to be free to attend, pending final event structure and approvals.

Will there be photography?

Yes, but privacy will be prioritized. Identifiable photography is consent-based, protected support areas are camera-free, and an opt-in photo area is planned. Read the full Photography & Media Policy.

Is the date and location final?

Not yet. April 24, 2027 and Main Plaza in Boerne are proposed and pending City approval.

Will support resources be available?

The plan includes survivor-service resources, counseling and advocacy support, legal and victim-service information, and medical support as partners are confirmed.

Will volunteers receive training?

Yes. Volunteer preparation is planned approximately two months before the event, with a final training/refresher approximately one month before. Volunteers will also receive a printed Code of Conduct and safety pamphlet. Exact dates will be announced after the event date and City approvals are finalized.
